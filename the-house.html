The house

One house, done right, before we build the next.

Free, certified sober housing for eight veterans in Clarksville, with a case manager and a clear path to a home of their own.

The need

Veterans who fall between programs.

34,000

veterans live in Clarksville and Montgomery County, the second-most of any county in Tennessee.

≈4,500

soldiers leave the Army at Fort Campbell each year.

31%

of them stay in the area, well above the Army average of 24%.

The gap we're filling

Most veteran transitional beds in the region require VA homeless-program approval and often have a wait. A veteran finishing addiction treatment, in Veterans Treatment Court, or just out of the Army can fall between programs: not homeless enough to qualify, and not stable enough to go it alone.

Who the first house is for

Men who have served, any branch and any discharge status, who are committed to sobriety and ready to work a plan. Many will come straight from residential treatment, a hospital stay, the courts, or their final weeks in uniform.

Figures: City of Clarksville; Fort Campbell transition briefings (2020–2022). Counts are approximate.

The first house

Small on purpose.

Eight beds lets us know every resident by name, prove the model, and earn the trust of the people who refer to us.

  • WhereClarksville, Tennessee, near Fort Campbell, the VA clinic, and bus lines
  • Size8 residents in a shared 5-bedroom home
  • ResidentsMale veterans to start; a women's house is part of the long-term plan
  • StandardSober living, certified through a Tennessee state-approved recovery residence program
  • StaffA dedicated case manager and a live-in house lead
  • StaySet by each resident's stability plan, typically six months to a year

What residents pay

Nothing.

No rent, no program fees, no deposit. A veteran working toward stability shouldn't have to choose between a bed and a bus pass. Residents' only job is to work their plan.

  • Funded by bed sponsors, donors, and community partners
  • Grants as we build a track record
  • Every dollar shown in our published budget

The path

From first call to your own front door.

Every resident will move through the same five stages. The pace is theirs; the direction is always forward.

  1. Day 1

    Referral

    A case manager, counselor, court, family member, or the veteran reaches out, and we follow up quickly.

  2. Week 1

    Intake

    A conversation to confirm fit, review needs, and check benefit eligibility.

  3. Move-in

    Land

    A furnished bed, house expectations, and a housemate who shows you the ropes.

  4. Months 1–6

    Build

    A written stability plan: income, benefits, health care, recovery, and housing goals.

  5. Graduation

    Move forward

    Into permanent housing, with follow-up support after move-out.

What residents will get

Housing is the foundation. Support is the structure.

Housing

Safe, sober, furnished

A bed in a shared home, utilities and internet included, and weekly house meetings that keep things steady.

Case management

One person in your corner

A case manager who knows your plan, your appointments, and what's next.

Benefits

VA and disability navigation

Help filing and following up on VA health care, disability claims, SNAP, and SSI/SSDI.

Employment

Work and income

Translating military experience into a résumé, job leads, and veteran-friendly local employers.

Recovery

Continuing care

Connection to outpatient treatment, peer groups, and mental health care at the VA and in the community.

Community

People who get it

Live alongside other veterans. Shared standards, shared meals, shared accountability.
